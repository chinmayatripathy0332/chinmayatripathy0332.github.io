Research

  • Sub-THz SAR imaging
  • Image rejection & heterodyne receivers
  • Wearable DSIL radar
  • Vital-sign sensing
  • 4D MIMO radar

I build sub-THz synthetic-aperture radar (SAR) imaging systems around 100 GHz, and I care about the receiver as much as the image: how the image-rejection ratio of a sub-THz heterodyne receiver shapes the Doppler signal you finally measure, and how to characterise it from the radar's own measurements.

At the other end of the scale I shrink radar onto the wrist: a 5.8 GHz delay- and self-injection-locked (DSIL) low-IF radar with a printed split-ring resonator that fits a smartwatch footprint and reads respiration and heartbeat without touching the skin. I am also closely involved in the lab's 4D MIMO radar work — range, velocity, azimuth and elevation from virtual arrays.

Cross-range resolution ≈ λR / 2L, range resolution ≈ c / 2B. The real rail-SAR runs at 100.9 GHz, where a 3 mm wavelength buys millimetre-scale detail.

Why image rejection matters for Doppler radar. A Hartley receiver cancels the image with a 90° phase shift; any I/Q amplitude or phase error lets a mirror tone leak through, and that leak bends the Doppler phase you measure into odd-order harmonics.

The EuMW 2026 wrist radar's Hartley image rejector measured 30.5 dB; the IMS 2025 paper shows how to read the IRR of a sub-THz receiver straight from the radar trajectory of a moving target.

How a 4D MIMO radar gets its angular resolution: N transmitters × M receivers behave like N·M virtual antennas. Add elements and watch the beam sharpen; drag the steering slider to point it. A second axis of elements does the same for elevation — that is the fourth dimension.
