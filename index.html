Research

  • Sub-THz SAR imaging
  • Image rejection in heterodyne receivers
  • DSIL wearable radar
  • Vital-sign sensing
  • 4D MIMO radar and ISAC

My main project is a 100.9 GHz FMCW synthetic aperture radar on a motorised rail. I measure point-spread functions with metal spheres to pin down range and cross-range resolution, then image letters, curved objects and multi-target scenes with omega-k processing. Part of that work is the receiver itself: measuring the image-rejection ratio of a sub-THz Hartley receiver with the radar's own moving-target echo, and showing how poor image rejection distorts the Doppler phase.

The second line is wearable radar. With the group I built a 5.8 GHz delay- and self-injection-locked (DSIL) low-IF radar with a split-ring resonator that fits a smartwatch charging-coil footprint and reads respiration and heartbeat from the wrist. I also work on the lab's 4D MIMO radar and ISAC platforms: a 6.4 GHz 8x8 TDM-MIMO FMCW radar for human pose and posture, and a 7.1 GHz MIMO-OFDM prototype that senses with communication signals.

A synthetic aperture forming an image. The antenna (amber) slides along the rail and every position adds one back-projected echo of three point targets. At first the targets are smeared into arcs; as the aperture grows they focus. Bandwidth sets range resolution, frequency sets cross-range resolution.

Cross-range resolution is about λR/2L and range resolution about c/2B. The real rail SAR runs at 100.9 GHz, where a 3 mm wavelength gives millimetre detail.

Why image rejection matters for Doppler radar. A Hartley receiver cancels the image with a 90° phase shift. Any I/Q amplitude or phase error lets a mirror tone through, and that leak bends the measured Doppler phase into odd-order harmonics.
